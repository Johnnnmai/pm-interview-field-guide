# From Consultant to Product Manager

Consultants follow a similar path to other business professionals transitioning to PM. Product managers focus more on execution and user outcomes than on advisory recommendations, so you need to build product craft and technical literacy first. But you have a unique advantage: you can structure any ambiguous problem and communicate solutions to any audience.

## What You Already Have

- Structured problem-solving and frameworks
- Client management and stakeholder communication
- PowerPoint, Excel, financial modeling
- Market research and competitive analysis
- Executive presence and presentation skills
- Cross-functional project management

## What You Need to Learn

### Product Craft

- Product sense - understanding what users want, not just what clients request
- User research - interviews, usability tests, contextual inquiry (different from market research)
- Prioritization frameworks - RICE scoring, opportunity sizing, ICE scoring
- PRD and spec writing - translating strategy into buildable requirements
- Agile execution - Scrum, sprint planning, backlog grooming, user stories
- Shipping discipline - seeing a product from spec to production to user feedback loop

### Technical Literacy

Two paths to choose from:

Path One: Data and Analytics (recommended to start here)
- SQL for querying databases and self-serve analytics
- Amplitude, Mixpanel, or Google Analytics for product metrics
- Pros: immediately applicable, every PM needs this
- Fit: those who want to be data-driven PMs at growth-stage or enterprise companies

Path Two: Technical Depth (strongly recommended for tech companies)
- How APIs work - REST, webhooks, GraphQL
- Basic system architecture - frontend, backend, databases, microservices
- Pros: earns credibility with engineering teams, enables better scoping
- Fit: those targeting PM roles at technical companies (AI, developer tools, infrastructure)

If you have time, do both. Analytics as your foundation, technical depth for credibility. SQL proficiency alone puts you ahead of 60% of PM candidates.

### User Research

- Qualitative methods (user interviews, diary studies, contextual inquiry)
- Quantitative methods (surveys, funnel analysis, cohort analysis)
- Synthesizing research into actionable product decisions

### Data and Metrics

- Metrics trees - breaking OKRs into leading and lagging indicators
- A/B testing and experiment design
- Dashboards - Looker, Tableau, Amplitude
- Unit economics - CAC, LTV, payback period

### Product Design Fundamentals

- Wireframing basics - Figma, enough to communicate ideas
- Design reviews - giving and receiving feedback on UX
- Information architecture and user flows

## Why This Transition Works

Consulting teaches you to structure ambiguous problems, synthesize data into recommendations, and present to senior stakeholders. These are core PM skills. The gap is that consulting recommendations end at the slide deck - PM work starts there. You need to learn how to take a recommendation through to shipped product, iterate based on user feedback, and measure real outcomes.

From consulting, you can transition to product strategy roles first and then move into execution-focused PM roles. Your advantage over other candidates: you can take an ambiguous problem, structure it into a clear plan, and communicate it to any audience from engineers to executives.

## Suggested Path

### If choosing the analytics-first route

1. Learn SQL and data analysis - the single highest-ROI skill for PM candidates
2. Learn product sense - product teardowns, user psychology, jobs-to-be-done
3. Practice user research - conduct real interviews, not just market research
4. Build a product - use no-code tools to prototype, test with real users
5. Learn agile execution - join a team using Scrum, write user stories, run sprints
6. Add technical literacy - APIs, system architecture basics
7. Deploy your consulting skills - stakeholder management, roadmap presentations

### If choosing the technical depth route

1. Learn how APIs and system architecture work
2. Learn SQL and database concepts
3. Build technical literacy through side projects or technical courses
4. Practice product teardowns and user research
5. Learn prioritization frameworks (RICE, opportunity sizing)
6. Join a product team in a strategy or associate PM role
7. Build execution skills through shipping real features

For a structured approach to PM interviews with 200+ real examples, check out [The 0->1 PM Interview Playbook](https://valenx.org/books/pm).

## Timeline

2-3 months to become interview-ready if you focus on SQL, product sense, and user research first. You already have the strategic and communication foundation - the main work is building product execution skills and technical literacy on top of that.

## Your Advantage

Structured thinkers with strong communication skills are always in demand for PM roles. You can take an ambiguous market problem, break it into testable hypotheses, and present a clear recommendation to leadership. Most PMs coming from technical backgrounds struggle with this kind of strategic communication. This makes you uniquely valuable, especially at companies navigating complex markets or enterprise sales.

Build your technical literacy early. Start with SQL and basic API concepts - practice with real datasets and build something small. Once technical concepts click, focus on product execution: shipping features, measuring outcomes, iterating based on real user behavior.
